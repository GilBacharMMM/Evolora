# [TOPIC 1: Reading: A Day at School]

## [TEXT]

Read the text carefully.

**A Day at School**

Tom usually walks to school. Today, he is riding his bike to school. His friend Emma plays basketball every morning. Right now, she is reading a book in the classroom. Their teacher usually writes on the board. At the moment, she is talking to the students.

## [QUESTION: multiple choice] [LEVEL 1] [NEW: suggested by Claude]

Which sentence from the text describes something that happens **regularly**?

- (✔) His friend Emma plays basketball every morning.
- Right now, she is reading a book in the classroom.
- Today, he is riding his bike to school.
- At the moment, she is talking to the students.

- Hint: Look for words that tell you something happens again and again, like "every" or "usually".
- Solution: "His friend Emma plays basketball every morning." The words "every morning" show that it happens regularly, again and again. The other sentences describe what is happening now or today.
- Why it's right: Correct. "Every morning" tells us it is a habit, something Emma does again and again.
- Tip: What does "regularly" mean? | Something that happens regularly happens again and again: every day, every week, or usually.

## [QUESTION: multiple choice] [LEVEL 1] [NEW: suggested by Claude]

- (✔) Right now, she is reading a book in the classroom.
- Tom usually walks to school.

- Hint: Look for words like "now", "right now" or "at the moment".
- Solution: "Right now, she is reading a book in the classroom." The words "right now" show that it is happening at this moment.
- Why it's right: Correct. "Right now" tells us the action is happening at this moment.
- Tip: Which words tell us "now"? | Now, right now, at the moment, today. They tell us something is happening at this time.

## [QUESTION: multiple choice] [LEVEL 2] [NEW: suggested by Claude]

Look at the verbs: "Tom **walks**" and "Tom **is riding**". What difference do you notice?

- (✔) The verb for now has "is" before it and ends with "-ing".
- The verb for now ends with "-s".
- The verb for a habit has "is" before it.
- There is no difference.

- Hint: Compare the two verbs letter by letter. Which one has an extra word before it?
- Solution: "Walks" is one word that ends with "-s". "Is riding" has two parts: "is" and a verb that ends with "-ing". That is how we talk about things happening now.
- Why it's right: Correct. Things happening now use am/is/are plus a verb with "-ing", like "is riding".
- Tip: What is "-ing"? | "-ing" is an ending we add to a verb: play, playing; read, reading.

- Solution: "Usually" means most of the time, again and again. So "walks" describes a habit: Tom walks to school most days.

# [TOPIC 2: When do we use each tense?]

## [TABLE]

| Present Simple | Present Progressive |
|---|---|
| Habits, daily routines, regular actions, facts and general truths. | Things happening now, actions in progress, temporary situations. |
| I brush my teeth every morning. | I am eating breakfast now. |
| I like chocolate. | I am eating an apple. |
| She plays tennis. | She is playing tennis. |
| The sun rises in the east. | They are watching TV. |

## [QUESTION: multiple choice] [LEVEL 1] [NEW: suggested by Claude]

"The sun rises in the east." Why is this sentence in the Present Simple?

- (✔) It is a fact, a general truth.
- It is happening right now.
- It is a temporary situation.
- It is an action in progress.

- Hint: Look at the table. Which column talks about facts and general truths?
- Solution: The sun always rises in the east. It is a fact, and facts use the Present Simple.
- Why it's right: Correct. Facts and general truths use the Present Simple.
- Tip: What is a general truth? | Something that is always true, like "Water is wet" or "Cats have four legs".

### Check-again question [LEVEL 1]

"Water freezes at zero degrees." Why is this sentence in the Present Simple?

- (✔) It is a fact, a general truth.
- It is happening right now.
- It is a temporary situation.
- It is an action in progress.

## [QUESTION: fill in the blanks] [LEVEL 1] [NEW: suggested by Claude]

Choose the correct tense for each sentence:

"I brush my teeth every morning" is in the ____ [*Present Simple, Present Progressive] because it is a daily routine. "They are watching TV" is in the ____ [*Present Progressive, Present Simple] because it is happening now.

- Hint: Routines and habits go in one column of the table; things happening now go in the other.
- Solution: Present Simple, Present Progressive. A daily routine uses the Present Simple. Something happening now uses the Present Progressive.
- Why it's right: Correct. Routines use the Present Simple, and actions happening now use the Present Progressive.
- Tip: What is a daily routine? | Something you do every day, like brushing your teeth or eating breakfast.

## [QUESTION: multiple choice] [LEVEL 1] [EXTRA] [NEW: suggested by Claude]

"I like chocolate." Which tense is this?

- (✔) Present Simple
- Present Progressive

- Hint: Is there "am", "is" or "are" with a verb ending in "-ing"?
- Solution: Present Simple. "Like" has no "am/is/are" and no "-ing". Liking something is a general truth about you, not an action happening right now.
- Why it's right: Correct. "I like" is a general truth about you, so it's in the Present Simple.

## [QUESTION: multiple choice] [LEVEL 1] [EXTRA] [NEW: suggested by Claude]

"I am eating an apple." Which tense is this?

- (✔) Present Progressive
- Present Simple

- Hint: Is there "am", "is" or "are" with a verb ending in "-ing"?
- Solution: Present Progressive: "am" + "eating". The action is happening now.
- Why it's right: Correct. "Am eating" is am + verb-ing, the Present Progressive.

# [TOPIC 3: How do we build a sentence?]

## [TEXT]

**Present Simple:** Subject + verb (+ s/es for he, she, it)

**Present Progressive:** Subject + am/is/are + verb-ing

## [TABLE]

| Subject | Present Simple | Present Progressive |
|---|---|---|
| I | play | am playing |
| You | play | are playing |
| He / She / It | plays | is playing |
| We / They | play | are playing |

## [QUESTION: multiple choice] [LEVEL 1] [NEW: suggested by Claude]

Which sentence is built correctly in the Present Progressive?

- (✔) He is playing football.
- He playing football.
- He is play football.
- He are playing football.

- Hint: The Present Progressive needs: subject + am/is/are + verb-ing. Check each part.
- Solution: "He is playing football." "He" takes "is", and the verb ends with "-ing". The other sentences are missing "is", missing "-ing", or use "are" with "he".
- Why it's right: Correct. He + is + playing: all three parts are there.
- Tip: Am, is or are? | I am. He, she and it: is. You, we and they: are.

### Check-again question [LEVEL 1]

Which sentence is built correctly in the Present Progressive?

- (✔) They are watching TV.
- They watching TV.
- They is watching TV.
- They are watch TV.

## [QUESTION: fill in the blanks] [LEVEL 1] [NEW: suggested by Claude]

Type the correct form of the verb **play**:

She ____ [plays] tennis every Sunday. Right now, we ____ [are playing] football.

- Hint: Look at the table. With "she" in the Present Simple, add "-s". With "we" in the Present Progressive, use "are" + verb-ing.
- Solution: plays, are playing. "She" takes "-s" in the Present Simple: she plays. "Right now" needs the Present Progressive, and "we" takes "are": we are playing.
- Why it's right: Correct. He/she/it add "-s" in the Present Simple, and we/you/they use "are" in the Present Progressive.
- Tip: When do I add "-s"? | In the Present Simple, add "-s" (or "-es") only after he, she or it: he plays, she watches.
- Tip: Am, is or are? | I am. He, she and it: is. You, we and they: are.

## [QUESTION: fill in the blanks] [LEVEL 1] [EXTRA] [NEW: suggested by Claude]

Choose the correct form:

I ____ [*am playing, is playing, are playing] the guitar now. They ____ [*are playing, is playing, am playing] in the garden.

- Hint: I goes with "am". They go with "are".
- Solution: am playing, are playing. "I" always takes "am"; "they" takes "are".
- Why it's right: Correct. I am, they are.

## [QUESTION: fill in the blanks] [LEVEL 1] [EXTRA] [NEW: suggested by Claude]

Choose the correct form:

He ____ [*plays, play, playing] chess every evening. You ____ [*play, plays, playing] the piano very well.

- Hint: In the Present Simple, only he, she and it add "-s".
- Solution: plays, play. "He" takes "-s": he plays. "You" doesn't: you play.
- Why it's right: Correct. Only he, she and it add "-s" in the Present Simple.

# [TOPIC 4: Time expressions]

## [TABLE]

| Present Simple | Present Progressive |
|---|---|
| Every day, usually, always, often, sometimes, every Monday, never | Now, right now, at the moment, Look!, Listen!, today*, this week* |

## [NOTE BOX]

**Be careful:** Today and this week can also appear in the Present Simple. Think about the meaning.

## [QUESTION: fill in the blanks] [LEVEL 1] [NEW: suggested by Claude]

Put each time expression in the right place. One word is extra.

We ____ eat pizza on Fridays. Look! The baby is sleeping ____. I ____ forget my homework, I always remember it!

- Words: usually, now, never
- Extra words: yesterday
- Hint: Which words go with habits, and which with now? "Yesterday" is about the past.
- Solution: usually, now, never. "We usually eat pizza on Fridays" is a habit. "The baby is sleeping now" is happening now. "I never forget my homework" is a habit too.
- Why it's right: Correct. "Usually" and "never" go with habits (Present Simple); "now" goes with things happening now (Present Progressive).
- Tip: What does "never" mean? | "Never" means not at any time. It describes a habit, so it goes with the Present Simple.

## [QUESTION: multiple choice] [LEVEL 1] [EXTRA] [NEW: suggested by Claude]

Which time expression goes with the Present Progressive?

- (✔) at the moment

- usually
- often

- Hint: Which expression means "now"?
- Solution: "At the moment" means now, so it goes with the Present Progressive. The others describe habits.
- Why it's right: Correct. "At the moment" means now.

## [QUESTION: multiple choice] [LEVEL 1] [EXTRA] [NEW: suggested by Claude]

Which time expression goes with the Present Simple?

- (✔) sometimes

- Look!
- at the moment

- Hint: Which expression describes how often something happens?
- Solution: "Sometimes" tells us how often something happens, so it goes with the Present Simple.
- Why it's right: Correct. Words that tell us how often (sometimes, always, never) go with the Present Simple.
